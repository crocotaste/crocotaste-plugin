# Questions

The guide for the conversation. Ask in this order, one per message, and skip what the user has already told you. Each entry has what we need to learn, a way to ask it, and what to listen for.

## 1. The product

- Learn: what it is and what it does, in one sentence.
- Ask: "What is the product, in a sentence?"
- Listen for: the category (tool, store, game, service) and the one job it does. A brand for a ledger is not a brand for a playlist app.

## 2. The people

- Learn: who uses it, how expert they are, how often.
- Ask: "Who uses it, and how often?"
- Listen for: experts or newcomers, daily or once a year, at work or at home. Experts tolerate density; newcomers need room.

## 3. The moment of use

- Learn: where and when the product is opened, on what screen, in what mood.
- Ask: "Picture someone opening it. Where are they, and what do they want in that moment?"
- Listen for: hurry or leisure, phone or desk, daylight or night. This decides contrast, size and how much a screen can hold.

## 4. The feel in three words

- Learn: three adjectives the user wants people to feel.
- Ask: "If people described it in three words, which would you want them to be?"
- Listen for: pairs that pull against each other (calm and bold). Name the tension back and ask which wins when they collide.
- If stuck, offer a menu: "Calm, exact, quiet? Warm, playful, round? Sharp, fast, technical? Or your own."

## 5. What they love elsewhere

- Learn: two or three products, sites, objects or places whose look they like.
- Ask: "Name a product or site whose look you love, any category."
- Follow up once: "What exactly do you love about it?" The answer is what we take (its density, its one color, its type), never the whole thing.

## 6. What they hate elsewhere

- Learn: looks they do not want.
- Ask: "And one look you dislike, or that would feel wrong for this?"
- Listen for: the generic (another blue SaaS dashboard), the trendy (glows, gradients), the competitor's look. These go into Rejected, with reasons.

## 7. What would make them wince

- Learn: the line not to cross.
- Ask: "What would make you wince if you saw it on this product?"
- Listen for: a color, a tone of voice, a kind of illustration, a font. It is often sharper than the answer to question 6.

## 8. Must-keeps

- Learn: what already exists and stays.
- Ask: "Is there anything we must keep: a logo, a color, a font, a name?"
- Listen for: an existing logo file (ask for its path, never copy one from the web), a licensed font (keep it, find an open fallback), a color from a parent brand.

## When to stop

Stop when you can write the brief's Product and people and Feel sections without guessing. If a later choice depends on something you never asked (the user's audience is color blind, the product must print), ask it then, one question.

## How to ask

- One question per message, short, with no preamble.
- Play back the last answer in one line before the next question.
- A menu of two to four options only when the user is stuck, always with "or your own".
- Never ask the user to choose hex values or font names cold. Show directions and let them react.
