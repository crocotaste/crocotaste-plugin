01

Foundations

What Sample is, how it should feel, and the three rules we reach for when two good ideas disagree.

The product

Sample is the booking calendar for independent clinics. It turns a busy week of appointments into one calm screen a receptionist can read at a glance.

The feel

  • Calm
  • Exact
  • Warm

Calm, because the people using it are busy. Exact, because a wrong time costs a patient a visit. Warm, because a clinic is a place of care.

Principles that decide ties

  1. The schedule is the hero

    Every screen gives the week the most room. Menus and settings step back until someone needs them.

  2. Clear before clever

    A plain label beats a smart icon. If a control needs a tooltip to be understood, it needs a better word.

  3. Say it once

    One primary action per screen, one way to do each thing, one name for each idea across the product.

Clear space

03

Color

A warm paper neutral, one deep teal for action and a terracotta accent used sparingly. Every color is a token, and every token has a dark value.

Roles

--color-primary

Primary

Teal is for the one action that matters on a screen and for the current place in the app. If everything is teal, nothing is.

--color-accent

Accent

Terracotta marks what is new or needs a look today. Never a button, never a status.

--color-surface

Neutrals

Warm paper greys carry the page, so the schedule reads before the chrome around it.

Tokens

Each chip shows the light value on the left and the dark value on the right, read from the tokens in this file.

Contrast, measured

Measured from the tokens when this page loads, with the WCAG 2 formula. Text needs 4.5:1; large text, icons and focus rings need 3:1.

A grotesque with a little warmth for headlines, a quiet sans for everything people read and click, and a mono for times and codes.

Reading

Most of what Sample says is short: a name, a time, a room. When it has to say more, it says it in plain sentences that a tired receptionist can read at the end of a long day. Lines stay under seventy characters, paragraphs under four lines, and numbers always use the mono face so they line up.

05

Spacing

Space steps in fours: tight inside a control, generous between groups, so the week breathes without wasting a row.

Scale

  • space-1
  • space-2
  • space-3
  • space-4
  • space-6
  • space-8
  • space-12
  • space-16

Space inside a control comes from steps 1 to 3, between controls from 4 and 6, between sections from 8 up. Never a number off the scale.

Radii

Small for checkboxes and badges, medium for buttons and fields, large for cards and dialogs. Full is for switches and avatars only.

Elevation

shadow-smSwitch thumbs
shadow-mdMenus, toasts, a hovered card
shadow-lgDialogs

A shadow means something floats above the page. Cards sit flat on a hairline until a pointer lifts them.

Borders

border-width on color-border: dividers, cards, tables
border-width-strong: focus rings, the selected tab, the current row

A hairline separates. A thicker line means focus or selection, and nothing else.

Iconography

Line icons on a 24 px grid, drawn with round ends, sized with the text they sit beside.

Stroke 1.75 at 24 px, round caps and joins, no fills. Icons take the color of the text beside them and always come with a word, except in a toolbar where the word is the accessible label.

09

Voice

Sample speaks like a good receptionist: warm, brief and certain.

Tone

  • We name the person, the time and the place, every time.
  • We stay calm when something fails and say what to do next.
  • We use the words a clinic uses, not the words a database uses.

10

Responsive

Sample runs on a front desk monitor and on a clinician's phone between rooms. The layout changes at three widths; the rules below never do.

Breakpoints

0 640 900 1200
  • 640 px

    Below 640 the week becomes a list of days and the menu folds behind a button.

  • 900 px

    From 900 the sidebar stays open and the day shows two clinicians side by side.

  • 1200 px

    From 1200 the week shows every clinician and the booking panel opens beside it.

Rules that never change

  • Every target is at least 24 by 24 px, and 44 on touch screens.
  • Focus is always visible on every control: a ring in the focus color, set off from the edge.
  • Text meets 4.5:1 and icons, large text and focus rings meet 3:1, in light and in dark, as measured in chapter 03.
